Source fact is different from project adoption. A Zomato, Uber Eats or DoorDash practice may be real for that platform. Its use in our project is still hypothetical. We do not combine platform rules into an asserted Swiggy SOP.

What this draft includes

12 Swiggy findings, product boundaries and refund estimates; 10 proposed terms; 24 proposed operational rules; 28 issue families; 16 open policy choices; and 14 acceptance-case specifications.

What “80% coverage” means here

We target the main restaurant-order complaint families. We do not have Swiggy complaint volumes, so 28 listed families cannot prove 80% of contacts are covered. A rare safety issue still needs a route regardless of frequency.

Measure later: contacts in issue families with a usable, tested route ÷ all in-scope contacts. Count unresolved/unmapped cases and repeat contacts consistently. Do not substitute a scenario-count percentage for volume coverage.

CRITICAL Wrong money, unauthorized action, unsupported decision, serious safety risk or false completion.

HIGH Complaint access, clarity, privacy, review ownership and fair handling.

OPTIONAL Enhancements after the core workflow works.

Read first: Part A, then the proposed terms and open-choice register. Use the operational rules and coverage map when deciding what to implement.

PART B · ALL PROJECT POLICIES HERE ARE HYPOTHETICAL

How to decide what belongs in the project

The wording below is concrete enough to review. Nothing is accepted merely because it appears in this draft.

CLASSIFICATION RULE

All project terms, formulas, controls and workflow choices in Part B are hypothetical—even when inspired by Swiggy wording or documented competitor practice.

T01–T10 are proposed plain-language terms. B01–B24 turn them into agent actions. H01–H16 isolate unresolved thresholds and capability choices. C01–C28 map issue families. V01–V14 specify checks; they are not executed tests.

Four decisions that must remain separate

What remedy fits?
Who can authorize it?
Has money moved?
Who bears the cost?

An appropriate customer remedy does not itself authorize execution. An approved refund does not prove credit. A merchant recovery cap does not approve a customer refund. A fault choice does not authorize a rider deduction.

What sources can and cannot do

Competitors demonstrate possible practices. They do not prove those practices are universal or suitable here. Foreign contracts, grocery guarantees and merchant settlement protections remain scope-specific.

Law is a reference constraint, not a discardable hypothesis. The reference notes identify applicable legal frameworks and future commencement dates. Those notes are not labelled Swiggy policy. Hypothetical drafting must still respect applicable law if the project ever operates.

Effort labels

Small = bounded wording/condition changes. Medium = connected fields and states. Large = a new model or substantial workflow/integration. Relative to the current HTML, not production delivery commitments.

The HTML version offers Pending / Accept / Revise / Discard selectors and export. These are local draft notes; they do not amend the PDF, publish terms, change Git or adopt policy for Swiggy.
