# Narrative and motion presentation

User correction, 9 November 2026: the case needs to read as an articulated piece
of writing and present its motion through mockups, rather than bare video
players. This is an editorial and presentation change to the case, not a new
research result or a redesign of the original application.

## Read-through diagnosis

The rendered article was read in full, with the visible text extracted into a
single document. Its opening, Research, Insights and Design repeated the same
purpose in slightly different words. Short labels, source cautions, question
lists and Changed/Intended benefit/Trade-off fields made the case sound like a
review worksheet. The page needed continuity, sentence rhythm and a specific
point of view, as well as source accuracy.

The rewrite opens with a delivery dispute, then develops the source question,
three-party investigation, actual layout changes, scenario exploration and the
problems revealed by the working prototype. The main paragraphs connect these
parts without inventing a personal epiphany or a user study. Detailed research,
policy and evaluation records remain available through native disclosures.
The four-version comparison now uses connected prose instead of three repeated
rubric fields. The five scenarios have a keyboard-operated reader, so each can
receive enough room for its account and recording.

## Mockups and motion

DeviceFrame provides a laptop or desktop shell. It preserves the source's 8:5
screen ratio. DeviceMotion places the clean recording inside that shell, with
play/pause, restart and fullscreen controls outside it. Playback is user-led,
pauses when the scene leaves view, and does not autoplay. Without JavaScript,
native video controls and source links remain available. Fullscreen restores
native controls and removes them on exit to the composed scene.

The main clips are fresh captures of the unchanged fictional prototype through
visible browser inputs. They exclude the old baked-in headlines and editorial
camera canvas, so text from the case is not displayed inside a pretend product
screen. The originals remain archived. The decision recording first reviews
both photos before contacting the restaurant, matching the admission-of-swap
branch described in the prose. That branch was visually checked in its source
frames. No application data, rules or UI files were edited for the captures.

The laptop/monitor shells, surfaces and shadows are new editorial compositions,
not photographs or evidence of deployed hardware. The site keeps DM Sans and
its existing paper, neutral and ink colours; the app keeps its original Inter.
The Meditimer device images are not involved in this component.

## Exports and inspection

`capture-swiggy-motion.mjs` can capture one selected clip and now prepares the
decision branch through its actual photo-review controls. The system FFmpeg
binary was reused for Playwright recording through a workspace-only runtime
path; no system configuration or credentials were changed.

`export-swiggy-device-gifs.mjs` deterministically seeks each video inside the
rendered device scene and exports an animated GIF. The GIFs include the actual
mockup presentation. Public MP4s, posters, GIFs and metadata are under
`public/portfolio/swiggy/device`. Source captures and frame sequences remain
outside Git. Provenance records their source and treatment.

The reference transfer uses the existing reviewed Bettina Malu/Little Otter
study's large device compositions and Drew's task-based decision accounts.
Those supply presentation mechanisms, not project facts or another designer's
voice. Here the material is the real coded prototype and its documented
iterations. The costs are added frame area and reduced apparent UI scale;
fullscreen, direct original files and text interaction steps retain access to
the details. Desktop and small-screen compositions must be inspected before
reporting delivery.
